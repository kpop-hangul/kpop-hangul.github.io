## 1. Song Overview & Korean Learning Guide

Climbing straight to **#3 on the Melon Top 100**, **'퇴사할게여 (Narr. 기안84)'** by **SOYEON (소연)** from her album *끝내주는 인생* has become an anthem for South Korea's modern workforce and youth culture. Featuring an eccentric and hilariously grounded narration by webtoon artist and TV personality **Kian84 (기안84)**, the track tackles the universal dream of corporate escape: *toesa* (퇴사 - resigning from one's job).

### Why This Song Is Ideal for Korean Learners
Unlike songs dense with abstract poetry or rapid-fire wordplay, '퇴사할게여' is a masterclass in **authentic spoken Korean (구어체)**. It showcases the exact speech styles, polite sentence endings, and casual nuances South Koreans use when venting their feelings, explaining reasons, and making big life decisions. 

* **Recommended Proficiency Level**: **Intermediate (Level 2)**
* **Standardized Benchmark**: **TOPIK II Level 3 / CEFR B1**
* **Prerequisites**: Familiarity with basic verb conjugation and polite informal endings (`-아/어요`).

## 4. Essential Grammar Deep Dive

### Pattern 1: Verb Stem + -(으)ㄹ게요 ("I will... / Let me...")
* **Grammar Formula**:
  * Vowel ending or `ㄹ` batchim: **`Verb Stem + -ㄹ게요`** (e.g., 가다 $\rightarrow$ 갈게요, 퇴사하다 $\rightarrow$ 퇴사할게요)
  * Consonant ending (except `ㄹ`): **`Verb Stem + -을게요`** (e.g., 먹다 $\rightarrow$ 먹을게요, 잡다 $\rightarrow$ 잡을게요)

#### How It Works in Korean Conversation
`-(으)ㄹ게요` expresses the speaker's voluntary intention or decision made **in consideration of or directly to the listener**. Unlike the general future prediction `-(으)ㄹ 거예요` ("I am going to...") or the rigid formal declaration `-겠어요`, `-(으)ㄹ게요` creates a polite conversational promise or notice. It can only be used with **first-person subjects**.

#### How It Works in the Lyrics
> **"퇴사할게요 아쉬울 거도 없거든요"**
> *(I will resign; I have no regrets, you see.)*

SOYEON addresses her employer and the world directly, declaring her final workplace decision with clear, conversational politeness.

* **Pedagogical Note on Title Spelling (`퇴사할게여`)**: In standard grammar, the ending is `-요` (`퇴사할게요`). In everyday Korean texting and mobile chat (KakaoTalk), replacing `-요` with `-여` or `-용` adds a cute (*aegyo*), lighthearted nuance. SOYEON deliberately chose `퇴사할게여` in the title to inject playful irony into a serious corporate life event!

#### 2 Real-Life Spoken Examples
1. **Hangul**: 제가 회의 끝나고 바로 전화할게요.
   * **Romanization**: Jega hoeui kkeutnago baro jeonhwahalgeyo.
   * **English**: I will call you right after the meeting finishes.
2. **Hangul**: 오늘 저녁은 제가 맛있는 거 살게요!
   * **Romanization**: Oneul jeonyeogeun jega masinneun geo salgeyo!
   * **English**: Dinner is on me tonight; I'll buy something delicious!

### Pattern 2: Verb/Adjective Stem + -거든요 ("You see, it's because... / ...you know")
* **Grammar Formula**:
  * Present: **`Stem + -거든요`** (e.g., 없다 $\rightarrow$ 없거든요, 많다 $\rightarrow$ 많거든요)
  * Past: **`Past Stem + -었/았거든요`** (e.g., 참았다 $\rightarrow$ 참았거든요, 없었다 $\rightarrow$ 없었거든요)

#### How It Works in Korean Conversation
`-거든요` is used to provide background context, reasons, or private facts that **the listener does not yet know**. In natural conversation, it functions like saying *"You see..."*, *"The thing is..."*, or *"...because, you know."* Ending a sentence with `-거든요` gives speech a lively, expressive, and explanatory feel.

#### How It Works in the Lyrics
SOYEON crafts the entire rhythmic build of the song with repeated `-거든요` endings:
* **아쉬울 거도 없거든요** *(Because I have zero lingering regrets, you know)*
* **열심히 살 생각 없었거든요** *(I never planned to work this hard in the first place, you see)*
* **수백 번을 참았거든요** *(Because I've already held it in hundreds of times)*
* **버킷리스트가 있거든요** *(The thing is, I have a bucket list!)*

This deliberate repetition mirrors an authentic heart-to-heart talk with a friend over a late-night meal.

#### 2 Real-Life Spoken Examples
1. **Hangul**: 저 내일 연차 썼거든요. 그래서 오늘 밤샘 게임 할 거예요.
   * **Romanization**: Jeo naeil yeoncha sseotgeodeunnyo. Geuraeseo oneul bamsaem geim hal geoyeyo.
   * **English**: You see, I took paid annual leave tomorrow, so I'm playing games all night tonight.
2. **Hangul**: 그 식당 가지 마세요. 지난주에 가봤는데 서비스가 정말 별로였거든요.
   * **Romanization**: Geu sikdang gaji maseyo. Jinanju-e gabwanneunde seobiseuga jeongmal byeolloyeotgeodeunnyo.
   * **English**: Don't go to that restaurant. I went there last week, and the service was really terrible, you see.

## 5. Pronunciation Secrets (연음 & 받침)

### Secret 1: Mandatory Fortis Tensification (된소리되기) in `-(으)ㄹ` Endings
* **Written**: `퇴사할게요`
* **Actual Spoken Pronunciation**: `[퇴사할께요]` / `[퉤사할께요]`

Many Korean learners pronounce `-(으)ㄹ게요` with a gentle, soft `[ㄱ]` sound because it is spelled with a single `ㄱ`. However, under standard Korean phonological rules, whenever the prospective modifier or ending `-(으)ㄹ` precedes a lax consonant (`ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅈ`), that consonant **must be pronounced as a tense double consonant (된소리)**: `[ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ]`.
* Another lyric example: **살 생각** *(thought of living)* $\rightarrow$ pronounced **`[살 쌩각]`**, NOT `[살 생각]`!

### Secret 2: Double Final Consonant Resyllabification (연음 & 자음군 단순화)
* **Written**: `없었거든요`
* **Actual Spoken Pronunciation**: `[업써꺼든뇨]`

Look at what happens across these five syllables:
1. **없- (`ㅄ` batchim) + -었- (vowel suffix)**: When a complex double batchim meets a vowel, the second consonant carries over (liaison). Here, `ㅅ` shifts and tensifies into `[ㅆ]`, yielding `[업써]`. 
2. **-었- (`ㅆ` batchim) + -거- (consonant prefix)**: When `ㅆ` meets `ㄱ`, the representative sound of `ㅆ` (`[ㄷ]`) causes the subsequent `ㄱ` to tensify into `[ㄲ]`, yielding `[업써꺼]`. 
3. **-거든요**: In natural colloquial speech, nasal harmonization gives `-거든요` the smooth rhythm of `[든뇨]`. Hence: **`[업써꺼든뇨]`**.

* **Bonus Liaison**: Look at **`문밖으로`** *(out that door)*: `문밖` ends in `ㄲ`. When followed by the particle `-으로`, `ㄲ` shifts directly to the next syllable: **`[문바끄로]`**, NOT `[문박으로]`.

## 6. Cultural Context & Lyric Nuance

### 1. The Korean "Toesa" (퇴사) Culture & Memes
In South Korea's fast-paced corporate society, **퇴사 (resignation)** was once viewed with apprehension or career stigma. However, for the modern **MZ Generation (MZ세대)**, *toesa* has been reframed as a courageous act of self-preservation and mental health prioritization. "Resignation Vlogs" (퇴사 브이로그) and "Resignation Memes" (퇴사짤) are viral trends.

SOYEON's lyric:
> **"모두 안녕히 계세요"** *(Goodbye to everyone)*

...is an unmistakable shout-out to Korea's most iconic resignation meme: the viral *Inuyasha* voice clip where the character Kagome floats into the sky shouting: *"Goodbye everyone! I am casting off all worldly shackles and bondage to seek my own happiness!"* (안녕히 계세요 여러분! 전 이 세상의 모든 굴레와 속박을 벗어던지고 제 행복을 찾아 떠납니다!).

### 2. "이팔청춘 (二八靑春)" & Existential Cheer
`이팔청춘` is a classic Korean four-character idiom (*사자성어*). Formed by $2 \times 8 = 16$, it historically signifies the sweet bloom of youth (the teenage years through the early twenties). SOYEON puts a humorous, modern spin on it: *"Even if we make a mistake in our prime youth, so what? In the end, we're all going to be grandmothers anyway!"* This existential cheerfulness gives listeners permission to fail, pivot, and prioritize joy.

### 3. Kian84's Reality Check Narration
Webtoon artist and broadcaster **Kian84 (기안84)** is celebrated across Korea for his unvarnished, free-spirited authenticity. His mumbling commentary in the song:
> **"너 너 이거 또 거짓말하네 음"** *(You... you're lying again, aren't you? Hmm)*

...acts as the voice of real life. Every office worker has daydreamed about traveling the world, writing novels, and quitting on a whim. Kian84’s interruption represents the affectionate cynicism of a colleague who knows you'll probably show up to work tomorrow after all!
