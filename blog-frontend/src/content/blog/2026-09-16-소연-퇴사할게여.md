## 1. Song Overview & Korean Learning Guide

Climbing straight to **#4 on the Melon Top 100**, **'퇴사할게여 (Narr. 기안84)'** is the standout title track from **소연 (SOYEON)**'s full-length studio album *끝내주는 인생* (*An Awesome Life*). Featuring deadpan, candid narration from Korea's famously unconventional webtoon artist and entertainer **기안84 (Kian84)**, the track became an instant anthem for weary corporate workers and dreamers seeking emancipation from daily drudgery.

### Why This Song Is Ideal for Studying Korean
Unlike traditional dance-pop tracks heavy on English buzzwords or highly figurative poetry, '퇴사할게여' is written almost entirely in **natural, everyday spoken Korean (구어체)**. It showcases how native speakers genuinely express resignation, frustration, and hopeful daydreams. By analyzing its lyrics, learners master:
- Conversational sentence endings used constantly in real-life Korean office and casual settings.
- Authentic workplace and lifestyle vocabulary that textbooks rarely cover.
- The nuanced boundary between formal politeness, playful aegyo, and inner monologue.

### Recommended Proficiency Level
- **Target Level**: **Intermediate (Level 2)**
- **Standardized Equivalents**: **TOPIK Level 3–4** | **CEFR B1–B2**
- *Note for Beginners*: The chorus hook is repetitive and infectious enough for high beginners to sing along, but intermediate learners will gain the most by analyzing the subtle conversational nuance of `-거든요` and `-ㄹ게요`.

## 2. Key Lyrics Breakdown (Hook & Chorus)

Here are 6 iconic lines from the verse and pre-chorus that showcase Soyeon's witty, unapologetic lyrical storytelling.

### Line 1
- **Hangul**: 퇴사할게요 아쉬울 거도 없거든요
- **Romanization**: Toesa-halgeyo aswiul geodo eopgeodeunyo
- **English Translation**: I'm going to quit my job; I have nothing to regret anyway.
- **Literal Breakdown**: 퇴사할게요 (퇴사하다 to quit one's company + -ㄹ게요 I will / promissory future) + 아쉬울 (아쉽다 to feel lingering regret + -(으)ㄹ future noun modifier) + 거도 (것 thing + -도 even/also) + 없거든요 (없다 to not exist/have + -거든요 you see / because).

### Line 2
- **Hangul**: 원래 이렇게 열심히 살 생각 없었거든요
- **Romanization**: Wollae ireoke yeolsimhi sal saenggak eopseotgeodeunyo
- **English Translation**: I never actually planned on living life working this hard anyway.
- **Literal Breakdown**: 원래 (originally / in the first place) + 이렇게 (like this / this way) + 열심히 (diligently / passionately) + 살 (살다 to live + -(으)ㄹ prospective modifier) + 생각 (thought / plan / intention) + 없었거든요 (없었다 did not have [past] + -거든요 you see / because).

### Line 3
- **Hangul**: 오래 했어요 수백 번을 참았거든요

- **Literal Breakdown**: 오래 (for a long time) + 했어요 (하다 to do + -았/었어요 did) + 수백 (hundreds of) + 번을 (times / instances + -을 direct object particle) + 참았거든요 (참다 to hold back / endure + -았/었 past marker + -거든요 you see / as a matter of fact).

### Line 4
- **Hangul**: 쿨하게 짐을 싸고 뒤돌아보지 않고 모두 안녕히 계세요

- **Hangul**: 그래 이팔청춘 후회하면 어때요

### Line 6
- **Hangul**: 결국 우린 모두 할머니가 될 거고

## 4. Essential Grammar Deep Dive

### Pattern 1: Verb / Adjective Stem + -거든요 ("You see, it's because...")

#### Grammar Formula
- **Present (Verbs & Adjectives)**: `Stem + -거든요`
- **Past Tense**: `Stem + -았/었거든요`
- **Nouns**: `Noun + (이)거든요`
- **Meaning**: Explains a reason, fact, or background context to the listener that they may not know, roughly equivalent to *"You see..."*, *"Because..."*, or *"As a matter of fact..."*.

#### How It Works in the Lyrics
Soyeon builds the emotional cadence of Verse 1 using three consecutive `-거든요` clauses:
> **"퇴사할게요 아쉬울 거도 없거든요 / 원래 이렇게 열심히 살 생각 없었거든요 / 오래 했어요 수백 번을 참았거든요"**

Instead of using objective causal conjunctions like `-아/어서` or `-(으)니까`, Soyeon chooses `-거든요` because it sounds conversational, intimate, and definitive. It delivers the punchline of her justification: *"I'm resigning—you see, I don't even have any lingering regrets!"*

#### 2 Real-Life Spoken Example Sentences
1. **오늘 회식 못 갈 것 같아요. 선약이 있거든요.**
   - *Oneul hoesik mot gal geot gat-ayo. Seonyagi itgeodeunyo.*
   - "I don't think I can make it to the team dinner tonight. You see, I already have an appointment."
2. **이 카페 자주 와요. 커피도 맛있고 조용하거든요.**
   - *I kape jaju wayo. Keopido masitgo joyonghageodeunyo.*
   - "I come to this café often. You see, the coffee is delicious and it's quiet."

### Pattern 2: Verb Stem + -(으)ㄹ게요 / -(으)ㄹ게여 ("I will / Let me [promising or informing]")

#### Grammar Formula
- **Consonant-ending stem**: `Stem + -을게요` (e.g., 먹다 → 먹을게요)
- **Vowel-ending stem**: `Stem + -ㄹ게요` (e.g., 퇴사하다 → 퇴사할게요)
- **Playful/Cute Internet Variant**: `-(으)ㄹ게여` (Substituting '여' for '요')
- **Meaning**: Expresses a speaker's willing decision, promise, or intention directed at the listener. Unlike `-(으)ㄹ 거예요` (a general declaration of future plans), `-(으)ㄹ게요` directly involves or addresses the listener: *"I will do this (for you / letting you know)."*

#### How It Works in the Lyrics
> **"퇴사할게요"** / **"퇴사할게여"**

Soyeon combines the Sino-Korean verb `퇴사하다` (to quit one's company) with `-ㄹ게요`. By announcing her resignation with `-ㄹ게요` instead of an abrupt proclamation, she gives the statement a crisp, polite, yet unshakeable decisiveness. In the song title, she changes the standard `-요` into the playful **`-여`**, mimicking cheeky messenger texts.

#### 2 Real-Life Spoken Example Sentences
1. **정리 다 끝나면 제가 바로 전화드릴게요.**
   - *Jeongni da kkeutnamyeon jega baro jeonhwadeurilgeyo.*
   - "Once I finish organizing everything, I will call you right away."
2. **오늘 먼저 퇴근할게요! 내일 뵙겠습니다.**
   - *Oneul meonjeo toegeunhalgeyo! Naeil boepgetseumnida.*
   - "I'll take off first today! See you tomorrow."

## 5. Pronunciation Secrets (연음 & 받침)

### 1. Nasalization (비음화) & Aspiration (격음화) in `막막하다` [망마카다]
In the introductory monologue narrated by Kian84, he says: *"슬기 말처럼 곧 막막하겠지"* (*"Just like Seulgi said, it'll probably feel bleak soon"*).
- **Written**: `막막하다`
- **Actual Pronunciation**: `[망마카다]`
- **Rule 1 (Nasal Assimilation)**: The first syllable ends in the stop consonant 받침 `ㄱ`. When followed by the nasal initial consonant `ㅁ`, `ㄱ` assimilates into the nasal velar `ㅇ` ([막막] → `[망막]`).
- **Rule 2 (Consonant Aspiration)**: The second syllable's 받침 `ㄱ` meets initial `ㅎ` in `하다`. When a plain stop (`ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ`) merges with `ㅎ`, it produces an aspirated sound (`ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅊ`). Here, `ㄱ + ㅎ` collapses into `ㅋ` ([망막하다] → `[망마카다]`).

### 2. Tensification (된소리되기) in `-(으)ㄹ게요` [할께요] and Aegyo Spelling `~게여`
- **Written**: `퇴사할게요`
- **Actual Pronunciation**: `[퇴사할께요]`
- **Rule**: In Korean phonology, following prospective/attributive `-(으)ㄹ`, any following plain consonant (`ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅈ`) is pronounced as a reinforced, tense double consonant (경음화/된소리되기). Therefore, even though `-게요` is spelled with a single `ㄱ`, it is always pronounced `[께요]`.
- **Spelling Twist**: Notice the song title uses `퇴사할게여` instead of `퇴사할게요`. In Korean texting culture, ending sentences in **`-여`** or **`-용`** instead of standard **`-요`** softens the tone with intentional cuteness (애교). Applying this lighthearted text slang to the grave act of resigning creates an ironic, cathartic comedic effect!

## 6. Cultural Context & Lyric Nuance

### 1. The Korean 'Toesa Craze' (퇴사 열풍) and 'Resignation in the Chest'
In South Korea, securing a permanent job at a reputable company has traditionally been considered the ultimate sign of success. However, long hours, hierarchical corporate culture, and intense pressure have ignited a massive generational counter-movement known as **퇴사 열풍 (the resignation craze)**. A common Korean office idiom is **"가슴속에 사직서를 품고 다닌다"** (*"Walking around with a resignation letter tucked in one's chest"*). Soyeon's track taps directly into this shared fantasy of breaking free from corporate shackles.

### 2. "이팔청춘 (二八靑春)" — The Prime of Youth
In the pre-chorus, Soyeon sings: *"그래 이팔청춘 후회하면 어때요"*.
- **Literal Roots**: `이(2) × 팔(8) = 청춘(16-year-old youth)`.
- **Cultural Meaning**: Historically derived from classical Sino-Korean literature describing young people at age sixteen, today it colloquially refers to anyone in the vibrant, golden peak of their youth. Soyeon uses it to say: *"We are young and alive—if we make a mistake and regret it, who cares? We have plenty of time to bounce back!"*

### 3. The "Romantic Grandma" Metaphor (낭만적인 할머니)
> *"결국 우린 모두 할머니가 될 거고 / 뒤돌아보면 오늘 하루는 It was a romantic day."*

Rather than framing retirement or aging as something depressing, Soyeon offers a refreshing, existential perspective: in fifty or sixty years, everyone grows old anyway. When looking back from old age, today's dramatic workplace resignation won't be remembered as a catastrophic career blunder; it will be cherished as an audacious, romantic adventure.

## 8. Sing-Along & Shadowing Study Tip

### Master Conversational Cadence with Shadowing
Soyeon's delivery in '퇴사할게여' is a goldmine for language learners because her cadence mimics **real spoken dialogue set to a bouncy, mid-tempo groove**.

1. **Step 1: Spoken Recitation (Slow Shadowing)**  
   Before trying to sing at full speed, read the lyrics of Verse 1 aloud as if you are actually talking to your boss or a close colleague:
   > *"퇴사할게요 (pause), 아쉬울 거도 없거든요 (drop intonation)."*
2. **Step 2: Connect the Tensification**  
   Make sure you pronounce the ending `-ㄹ게요` with a crisp double-k sound: **`[퇴사할께요]`**. Do not let your vocal cords relax into a soft English 'g'.
3. **Step 3: Capture the Cheeky Ending**  
   Notice how Soyeon ends each phrase with a bright, bouncing upward inflection on **`-거든요`** (`[거-든-뇨]`). Mimicking this exact musical pitch will make your spoken Korean sound instantly more natural, expressive, and confident!
